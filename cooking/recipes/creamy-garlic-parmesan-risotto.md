# Creamy Garlic Parmesan Risotto

Rich, garlicky, and parmesan-packed — ready in about 30 minutes with basic pantry ingredients.

## Ingredients

- 4 cup vegetable or chicken stock
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tbsp butter (salted)
- 1/2 piece medium onion, finely diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 cup arborio rice
- 1/4 cup dry white wine
- 1 cup parmesan cheese, freshly grated (plus extra for serving)
- 3 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped

## Steps

1. Pour 4 cup vegetable or chicken stock into a small saucepan and warm over low heat. Keep it hot throughout cooking — cold stock cools the pan and affects texture.
2. In a large skillet over medium heat, melt 1 tbsp butter (salted) with 1 tbsp olive oil. Add 1/2 piece medium onion, finely diced and cook until just tender, about 3–4 minutes. Add 4 cloves garlic, minced and cook 1 minute more.
3. Add 1 cup arborio rice and stir to coat every grain with the butter and oil. Cook for about 1 minute.
4. Pour in 1/4 cup dry white wine and stir until fully absorbed. If skipping wine, add a small splash of stock instead.
5. Add one ladleful of hot stock at a time, stirring regularly and letting each addition absorb before adding the next. Maintain a gentle simmer — not a full boil. Continue for 17–25 minutes until the rice is al dente.
6. With the last ladle of stock, stir in 3 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped. Let the stock absorb halfway, then remove from heat and stir in 1 cup parmesan cheese, freshly grated (plus extra for serving). Let it finish absorbing until creamy and thick but not soupy.
7. Plate immediately and top with extra parmesan and a sprinkle of parsley if desired. If it thickens while sitting, stir in a splash of warm stock to loosen.

## Notes

**Wine:** Use something you'd actually drink — the flavor concentrates as it reduces. Skip it entirely and use extra stock if preferred.

**Arborio rice is key:** Its high starch content is what creates the creamy texture. Carnaroli also works great. Avoid long-grain varieties.

**Grate your own parmesan:** Pre-grated doesn't melt the same way and the flavor isn't as good.

**Texture fixes:** Too thick? Splash of warm stock. Still firm in the center? Keep adding stock and cooking. Too thin? Let it simmer a few more minutes.

**Storage:** Best served fresh. Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container and reheat with a splash of stock or water. Do not freeze.
